The new thing

A variable is a name that holds a value

use one when
  • the number has to change while the sketch is running
  • the same number appears in several places
in p5 that means
  • anything that moves
  • a size or color used by more than one shape

You have already used two without knowing: width and height.

TIME 4 min SAY "A name that holds a value." Not "a box" - a name is closer to the truth and it is the CED's language. Ask for examples of each reason before revealing the right column. Last line lands well: width and height have been in their code since Lesson 1 and they never thought of them as variables. WATCH FOR Nothing to watch - no code on this slide, deliberately.
Writing one

Give the number a name


let y = 100;

function draw() {
  background(24, 38, 60);
  circle(200, y, 60);
}
let makes a new name. y is the name. 100 is what is in it.
Change 100 to 300 The circle moves. You edited one number, not the circle line.
TIME 3 min SAY let makes a name, you pick the name, the number goes in it. Type it on the projector, change 100 to 300, run. The point is that the circle line never changed. Nothing moves yet. A variable before it is animation - that separation matters. WATCH FOR let written inside draw(). It would be created fresh sixty times a second and never change. Today just say "outside" and move on.

Together · you talk, I type

Make the antenna bulb slide


let bulbY = 70;

function draw() {
  background(24, 38, 60);
  fill(230, 90, 90);
  circle(200, bulbY, 26);
  bulbY = bulbY + 1;
}
bulbY becomes bulbY plus one Not “equals”. It is an instruction, not a claim.
TIME 4 min SAY Ask a student to read the last line aloud before you press. Whatever they say, then give them the wording on the callout. "Becomes", not "equals". Every time, all year. Ask what will happen before you run it - some will say it moves once. WATCH FOR Students reading = as "equals". They get stuck on this line for weeks if it is not corrected now.

Together · the fix

Measure everything from one point


fill(190, 200, 215);
rect(200, headY, 160, 150);

fill(70, 130, 200);
circle(165, headY - 25, 34);
circle(235, headY - 25, 34);

fill(40, 50, 70);
ellipse(200, headY + 42, 90, 30);
Not at 165 At 25 above wherever the head is. That distance never changes.
TIME 4 min SAY Fix it live, one shape at a time, running after each. Ask for the numbers rather than giving them: "the eyes were at 165 and the head was at 190. How far apart?" Twenty-five. "Above or below?" Above, so minus. Say it twice: not "at 165" but "at 25 above wherever the head is". Spare thirty seconds? headY - 1 and the whole robot rises intact. WATCH FOR Students copying the numbers without seeing where they came from. Ask one of them to derive the mouth offset out loud.
Now it matters

draw() runs about 60 times every second


bulbY = bulbY + 1;
One line. Sixty times a second.
That is 60 pixels of movement per second.
Change the 1 to a 4 Four pixels per frame. Four times faster.
TIME 4 min SAY Ask first: "we wrote one line. Why did it move more than once?" The table is the answer - draw() runs over and over, and every pass adds one more. Change 1 to 4 on the projector. Speed is just how much you add. Delete background() live and show the trail. Five seconds, worth it. WATCH FOR Held back since Lesson 1 on purpose. If this lands, the lesson landed.

Working out an offset The window sits at 125. landerY starts at 40.
TIME 9 min SAY Step 1 is landerY = landerY + 2 at the bottom of draw(). Step 2 happens on its own - the window and legs hang in mid-air. The robot's face again, on their own code. Step 3: window sat at 125, landerY starts at 40, so the offset is 85. Legs sat at 205, so 165. Worked examples are directly above. The pad does not move, and that is correct. WATCH FOR The new line put OUTSIDE draw(). Runs once, nothing moves. let landerY moved INSIDE draw(). Resets every frame - symptom is "nothing happens" and the cause is four lines away. Offsets computed from 0 instead of 40, giving landerY + 125. Window sits forty pixels too low, forever. Plausible and wrong. IF BEHIND Early finishers do faster, slower, upward. Going up means subtracting - first student to work that out says it to the room.
