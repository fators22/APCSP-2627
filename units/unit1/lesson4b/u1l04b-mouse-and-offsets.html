Yesterday, in three lines

How you write a variable

Use one when
  • the number has to change while the sketch runs
  • the same number appears in several places
Where it goes
  • let at the top, outside draw()
  • changing it goes inside draw()
SIX MINUTES. This is review, not new material - but it is worth the time because yesterday moved fast and half of them typed without reading. THE THREE PARTS: let makes a name, you pick the name, the number goes in it. Point at each color. THE SECOND HALF IS THE PART THEY DO NOT HAVE YET. Ask when you would bother with a variable at all. Two reasons, and the second one is the one they have not met: if the same number shows up in five places, a variable means you change it once. That is what today is about. Say the placement rule out loud again: let at the top, changing it inside. Inside draw() the let is created fresh sixty times a second and nothing ever grows.

THREE MINUTES. ASK. DO NOT ADVANCE. Someone will get the first question almost immediately - put the mouse position in there. Good. Do not confirm the name of it yet. The answer

p5 keeps two numbers for you

mouseX
mouseY
Where the cursor is, right now. Updated every frame, before draw() runs.
Same as yesterday headY was a point you changed. These are a point p5 changes.
THREE MINUTES. Type circle(mouseX, mouseY, 40) live and move the mouse. That is the whole demo and it takes ten seconds. THE CALLOUT IS WHY THIS IS A LESSON AND NOT A TRICK. Yesterday they made a variable and changed it themselves. Today the variable already exists and something else changes it. Same idea, different owner - and both are just names for numbers. You never write to mouseX. p5 sets it before every frame. If somebody tries mouseX = 200, it will not stick, and that is worth mentioning only if it comes up.

On the AP exam

The same idea, written differently

JavaScript — what you write

let armX = mouseX - 48;

let total = a + b;
Exam Reference Sheet
armX ← mouseX - 48

total ← a + b

No let, no semicolon. The arrow does the work.

TWO MINUTES. Second time they have seen this - it should go faster than yesterday. The arithmetic on the right side is identical. Only the assignment changes: an arrow instead of an equals sign, no let, no semicolon. ASK THEM TO READ ONE ALOUD. "armX becomes mouseX minus 48." If they can say it, they can read it in May. Do not drill it. Exposure now, practice in Unit 3 when the exam gets close enough to matter.
